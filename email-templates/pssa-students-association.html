Choseno is Canada's social network for politics — think of it as business reviews, but for politicians. Just like you research restaurants before dining, voters deserve to research candidates before elections. It gives voters a simple way to find their district, see who represents them at every level, and compare candidates side-by-side. We're launching for the 2026 municipal election cycle.

After speaking with a few political science majors who were all very excited about the platform, I wanted to reach out directly to your team. Please share this with your members and invite interested students to join us. This is a mission—and we need people who are passionate about giving voters the information they deserve before voting day. If you have questions or want to coordinate a presentation to your members, I'm happy to hop on a call.

What You'll Actually Do

You'll conduct structured interviews with municipal candidates running in 2026. Your research goes directly to voters who are trying to understand who represents them. This is real journalism meets political science—hands-on research that matters.

  • Conduct Interviews: 4-6 candidate interviews over 8 weeks (we provide the template & training)
  • Map Policy Positions: Help voters understand where candidates actually stand
  • Direct Access: Network with municipal candidates, campaign strategists, and election officials
  • Your Name, Your Impact: Featured on Choseno as the Election Researcher for your area—get recognized for the work you're doing

Why This Matters for You

  • Apply What You Study: Political theory isn't abstract—you're applying it to real elections happening right now
  • Build Your Portfolio: Co-author research case studies you can show employers or grad schools
  • Work with the Founder: Direct mentorship and collaboration with Murugappan Valliyappan—this isn't a typical volunteer gig, you're building something from day one
  • Be the First: You're the inaugural cohort launching Canada's civic research platform. This is historic. You're not following a playbook—you're writing it
